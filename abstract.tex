Early software fault prediction helps optimize resources and time during the testing and maintenance stages. The supervised machine learning models used for this task are trained on code-metric datasets that suffer from two persistent problems: high dimensionality and class imbalance. This thesis evaluates whether a two-stage preprocessing ---feature selection through relevance analysis and redundancy control, followed by class balancing--- improves fault prediction on BugHunter, a dataset of 15 Java projects with metrics at three levels of granularity (file, class and method).

Information Gain, Chi-Square and ReliefF are used for relevance analysis; Symmetric Uncertainty and Cosine Similarity for redundancy control; and Random Over-Sampling, Random Under-Sampling and SMOTE to balance the training set. Models are evaluated with Random Forest in 13,916 runs, complemented by a sensitivity analysis of 28,440 runs with Random Forest, XGBoost and a linear SVM that records per-class metrics.

Feature selection reduces the median number of metrics by 40\,\%, 88\,\% and 50\,\% at the method, class and file levels, respectively, with an F-measure loss below 0.02, and code-size metrics (CLOC, LOC and LLOC) are the most frequently selected. The most relevant finding concerns class balancing: applied only to the training set, it does not improve the weighted F-measure when the test set keeps the real class distribution (Cliff's $|\delta| < 0.15$), but it consistently improves the detection of the defective class (bug-class F-measure and MCC) across the three classifiers, a benefit that the aggregate metric masks. Training on individual projects outperforms training on the unified set of projects. The thesis also reviews the state of the art of large language models (LLMs) in defect prediction and evaluates their application through an exploratory pilot: three LLMs applied directly to the metrics do not outperform Random Forest in MCC and reproduce the same metric dependence. The results indicate that the conclusion about the usefulness of class balancing depends critically on the evaluation metric, a methodological caveat that extends to language-model-based approaches.
